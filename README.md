# Explaining SRL predictions via training-data attribution with label-distribution centroids
A practical approach to explain SRL decisions through label-centric retrieval of training examples and influence scoring.
This repository provides the implementation of a technique for training-data attribution in Semantic Role Labeling (SRL) models that identifies influential training tokens by modeling their relationship to the label-wise centroid in the representation space, enabling post-hoc retrieval of training examples without modifying or retraining the model.
Training-data–based explanations play an important role in understanding how NLP models produce predictions, especially in application settings that demand transparency, such as biomedicine or finance. However, existing methods still struggle to characterize the actual influence of individual training examples because determining whether an example is helpful or harmful often relies on counterfactual parameter updates rather than the model’s true parameter state. Some approaches additionally require architectural modifications or interventions in the training pipeline. This work introduces a new method grounded in the notion of label-distribution centroids, which model relationships among tokens of the same label in the representation space. Building on this structure, we propose an influence-polarity measure that quantifies both the direction and magnitude of each training example’s effect without retraining or modifying the training process. Experiments show that the proposed approach effectively retrieves highly influential training examples and clearly distinguishes supportive versus disruptive influence on model predictions. These findings support the proposed approach as a practical post-hoc tool for auditing token-level model behavior when retraining or architectural intervention is infeasible.
